# Landing pages and product copy

Marketing copy is where slop is most common and does the most damage. A visitor compares you with three other tabs. If your hero line could sit on any of their pages, you lose the comparison by default. Specific copy wins because it is the only kind a reader can check.

## What visitors need

- The category and the outcome in the headline: what it is, and what changes for them.
- Who it is for. Naming the audience also tells the wrong audience to leave, which is fine.
- Evidence near every claim: a number, a customer name, a screenshot, a price.
- What it costs and what the next step is.
- Answers to the objections they actually have (migration, lock-in, security, price).

## Patterns to avoid

- Hero lines built from empty verbs: `Unlock your potential`, `Elevate your workflow`, `Supercharge your team`, `Empower creators` (`vocab/empty-verbs`).
- Superlatives with nothing behind them: `world-class`, `cutting-edge`, `state-of-the-art`, `unparalleled`, `revolutionary` (`vocab/hype-adjectives`).
- Impact inflation: `game-changer`, `paradigm shift`, `take your business to the next level` (`vocab/game-changer`).
- Scene-setting openers: `In today's fast-paced digital landscape` and `Imagine a world where` (`opener/fast-paced-world`, `opener/ever-evolving-landscape`, `opener/imagine-a-world`).
- Problem hooks in question form: `Tired of juggling spreadsheets?` (`opener/rhetorical-hook`). State the problem as a sentence.
- Feature grids of three adjectives each, repeated down the page: `Fast. Simple. Secure.` (`structure/rule-of-three`).
- Fake suspense: `The secret? Automation.` (`structure/colon-reveal`).
- Board-meeting nouns: `synergy`, `holistic`, `actionable insights`, `best-in-class`, `one-stop shop` (`vocab/corporate-speak`).
- Checkmark emoji as bullets and exclamation marks as default punctuation (`format/emoji-bullets`, `format/exclamation-overuse`).
- Closers like `The possibilities are endless` (`closer/future-is-bright`).

## Instead, do this

- Write the headline as a claim a competitor could not copy. If they could paste it on their site unchanged, rewrite it.
- Attach a proof to each claim on the same line: "Imports a 2 GB CSV in 11 seconds", "Used by the data team at a 400-person fintech".
- Describe features as things the user does: "Search every invoice by amount, vendor, or date", not "Powerful search".
- Use the customer's vocabulary. If they say "close the books", don't write "streamline financial operations".
- Put the price, or the pricing model, on the page.
- Write one call to action per section and make the button say what happens: "Start a 14-day trial", not "Get started".
- Keep at most one exclamation mark on the whole page.

## A quick test

Read each sentence and ask "compared to what?" and "how would I check that?". If neither has an answer, the sentence is decoration.

## Before and after

Before:

```text
Revolutionize Your Finances!
In today's fast-paced world, managing money is harder than ever. Imagine
a world where budgeting is effortless. Our cutting-edge, AI-powered
platform empowers you to unlock financial freedom.

✅ Seamless syncing
✅ Actionable insights
✅ Bank-level security

The best part? It's free!
```

After:

```text
Budgeting that updates itself
Ledgerly imports transactions from 9,000 banks every night and sorts
them into the categories you set. Most people spend five minutes a week
on it instead of an hour with a spreadsheet.

Read-only bank access through Plaid. We never move money.
Free for one account; $6 a month for unlimited accounts.

[Connect a bank, takes about two minutes]
```

The second version names the product, the mechanism, the time saved, the security model, and the price. Each line answers a question a buyer would ask.
